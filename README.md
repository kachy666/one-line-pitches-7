> My daughter drew her homework app on the back of a maths worksheet; I read it to make.fun, and by bedtime she was tapping the real thing.

The pitch's angle is a concrete scene, not a description of the platform: a kid's homework app, drawn on the back of a maths worksheet, read to make.fun — and by bedtime she is tapping the real thing. If a child's drawing becomes a working app before bedtime, a friend who has never coded has no excuse left to keep their own idea on paper.

Why this angle, over the four others written for this round: the investors-demo line ("Investors want a demo, not a deck...") speaks to a market, not a friend; the graveyard line ("Your phone is a graveyard of apps that died as meeting notes...") is clever but abstract; the 3am-coffee line has a vivid scene and a weak claim; the freelancer-quote line compares prices instead of showing the thing working. The kid's drawing wins on the judge's own order — persuasion first: if a drawing works, any idea works — and on warmth and craft second: it is the only one of the five that sounds like a person talking to a friend.

Run it with:

```sh
python3 main.py
```

It validates the pitch (one sentence, at most 240 characters) and writes it to `/output/entry.txt`.
